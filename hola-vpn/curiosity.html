Key points

  • Websites read your IP address to estimate your country.
  • Many sites change what they show, or whether they load at all, based on that estimate.
  • A free desktop app lets you choose the country yourself.

Type the same web address in New York and in London and you may not get the same page. Not a different design: different content, a different library, sometimes a polite note that the page isn't offered where you are.

The reason is a number attached to every connection, your IP address. Sites use it to estimate your country before the page even loads. Most people never realize how much of what they see is decided by it.

You've probably noticed it without knowing

You look up something you saw while you were traveling, and back home it's gone. Or a friend abroad mentions a show, a shop or an article, and when you open the link it says it isn't available.

You assume it was taken down. Often it's still there. It just isn't shown to connections from your country.

Licensing deals, local laws and business choices all play a part. The result is that there isn't really one internet. There are many versions of it, sorted by location.
