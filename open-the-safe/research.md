### "GTA style": trademark caution
"GTA" / Grand Theft Auto is a Rockstar Games / Take-Two trademark. The offer's own game is styled after it,
but **our pages and ads never say GTA, never use its logo, characters, fonts, art or map look**, and never
suggest the game is made or endorsed by Rockstar. Using a game brand in native ads is a fast way to get
creatives rejected or a trademark complaint. The theme on our side is a neutral "crack the safe" puzzle,
not crime: no robbery, guns, police or money piles.

### Adsterra policy check: allowed, with restrictions
Adsterra supports sweepstakes / giveaway offers on native and display. Creative rules: nothing misleading,
no malware, no forced redirects. Our baseline (same as the other giveaway campaigns):
- No "You won!", "Congratulations", fake alerts or notification UI; no guaranteed prize, no prize or peso
  amount, no cash imagery; no countdowns; no bank, telco, e-wallet, store or game-studio impersonation.
- Every page: chance to win, no guaranteed prize, sponsor's official rules, a real giveaway never asks you
  to pay to collect a prize. Native line in the footer; "Advertisement · Advertorial" label.
- Check Zeydoo's offer page for this offer's allowed traffic types before launch.

### Adsterra Native Banner creative specs (from the user's Adsterra skill / Adsterra's guide)
Image 320×240 JPG/PNG (4:3), ≤ 350 KB; title ≤ 64 characters. Adsterra advice: people in the image, one large
object on a contrasting background, uncluttered; the image drives most of the result.

## 2. Avatar
1. **Who:** Filipino adults 18–35, mostly men but many women, on Android with prepaid data. Students, BPO
   agents, service and retail workers, commuters. Long daily commutes (jeepney, van, MRT), long queues,
   night shifts; the phone is their entertainment. Mobile gaming is huge in PH (MOBA, casual, open-world).
2. **Top 10 pain points:**
   1. Hours lost in traffic and queues every day.
   2. Boredom in short gaps; wanting something quick, not a long game.
   3. "Free" games full of ads, pop-ups and paywalls.
   4. Fake "you won" texts and links asking for a fee or OTP.
   5. Scams that pretend to be banks, telcos, e-wallets or stores.
   6. Giveaways that become endless surveys.
   7. Slow pages that eat prepaid data.
   8. Not knowing who runs a promo.
   9. Tight budget: anything free and fun is welcome.
   10. Feeling that only other people ever win.
3. **Already tried:** casual and puzzle games with heavy ads, Facebook "share to win" posts, raffle apps,
   spin-the-wheel sites, survey sites.
4. **What makes them click now:** a simple challenge ("can you open the safe?"), the itch to crack a code,
   free, about a minute, and an honest page.
5. **Where they spend time online:** Facebook and Messenger, TikTok, YouTube, free streaming / anime /
   download sites (where Adsterra native widgets run), mobile games.

## 3. Angle scores
| Angle | Score | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Curiosity | 5 | "Can you open the safe?" is a natural open loop; picking a safe is almost the game itself. |
| Direct Benefit | 4 | Clear end state: a free one-minute game and a chance to win. |
| Pain Point | 3 | Boredom in traffic and fake prize links are real, but weaker than for a seasonal theme; an honest story still builds trust on native. |
| Fear | 1 | No factual fear behind a safe game; scam-fear contradicts sending people to a giveaway. |
| Social Proof | 1 | No winner numbers or reviews from the offer owner. Guardrail: no proof → skip. |

**Picked:** Curiosity (5), Direct Benefit (4), Pain Point (3). **Skipped:** Social Proof (guardrail), Fear (no factual basis).
