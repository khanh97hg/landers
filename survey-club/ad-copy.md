# Ad Copy: Survey Club - CP1C (Facebook, iOS, US + CA)

Traffic source: **Facebook (Meta: Facebook + Instagram)**, one ad set per angle, $50 split equally (~$16.67 each), each pointing at its own page.
Headlines are 40 characters max and descriptions 30 max (checked). Hooks are under 15 words.

**Before launch:** target **iOS devices only** and **US + Canada** in every ad set (the offer is iPhone-only). Meta income-claims rules: no amounts, no "make money" or "easy cash" promises, no cash or payout-screenshot images, and no lines that assert facts about the viewer's finances ("Need extra cash?"). Every line below describes the product or a scenario. No Special Ad Category applies.

## Pain Point (Advertorial)

**Landing page:** https://khanh97hg.github.io/landers/survey-club/pain.html

**Image ads:** `ads/img/pain-feed-4x5.webp`, `ads/img/pain-feed-1x1.webp`

### Hooks

1. Twenty questions in, then "you don't match." Sound familiar?
2. Survey apps feel frustrating for a reason. Here's what fixes most of it.
3. One screen-out shouldn't end your whole survey session.

### PAS body

Survey apps promise "about 5 minutes," then screen you out twenty questions in. A few points land in your account, you can't tell what they're worth, and there's nothing else waiting, so the session ends. A free iPhone app that lists paid research studies from many sources in one place changes that: a screen-out just means trying the next one, and rewards go out by PayPal or gift card. Free to join. You won't qualify for every survey.

### Meta / Facebook: primary text

**Short:** Screened out after twenty questions? An iPhone app with many studies in one list means there's always a next survey to try. Free to join.

**Medium:** Most survey frustration isn't you. Each study looks for a specific group of people, so screen-outs happen on every app. The difference is what comes next: with many research studies in one list, there's another one to try. Rewards by PayPal or gift card. Free on iPhone, no card needed.

**Story (second-person scenario):** You've got twenty minutes on the train, so you open a survey app. It says "about 5 minutes." Twelve minutes later you're still rating laundry detergent scents. Then: "Unfortunately, you do not qualify." A few points, no idea what they're worth, and nothing else to try. There's a less frustrating setup: a free iPhone app that lists paid studies from many sources, so a screen-out just means the next one, with rewards by PayPal or gift card. You won't qualify for every survey. Nobody does.
